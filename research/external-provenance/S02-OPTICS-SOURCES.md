## Research boundaries and open proof requirements

- The sources explain proven mathematics and available renderer controls. None establish that MGR currently emits physically calibrated light, reconstructs material properties from one image, or achieves film-quality output.
- Blender release-specific settings and color management must be verified on the actual Windows installation; documentation URLs are versioned where available.
- A valid proof suite needs measured or analytic expected values, captured inputs/outputs, deterministic scene files, versioned color transforms, tolerance bands and failure cases. Subjective similarity may complement but never replace geometric/physical metrics.
- Physically grounded evaluation must distinguish point-source inverse-square falloff in its proper regime, area-source near-field behavior, and renderer-specific semantics; simplistic brightness ratios can mislead under tone mapping or indirect illumination.
- **Licensing:** cite and study sources; never copy protected commercial code or constrained material datasets under the label “native.” Native algorithms should be separately authored and independently tested.

## Targeted follow-up verification

1. Inspect the exact runtime Blender 5.2.x API and render outputs for fixture powers, falloff, exposure, light-linking and color view transforms; record builds and samples.
2. Run a controlled sphere/gray-card/checkerboard/light-falloff suite under both Standard and AgX/ACES-managed transforms, retaining scene-linear EXR before display conversion.
3. Integrate calibrated intrinsics/distortion and renderer setting fingerprints into the MGR camera/lighting proof records; reject “validated realism” without control witnesses.
4. For materials, compare synthetic BRDF families first, then obtain specifically licensed measured reflectance sets or create MGR-owned capture data.
